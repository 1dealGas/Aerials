### Plot

The girl's name was Nerin.

Trapped in a void of endless white, she felt as though the endless cold and loneliness had completely consumed her. She strained to open her eyes, but there was nothing but white. She tried to speak, but her throat clenched as if choked by invisible hands. Reaching out, her fingers grasped only frigid air.

Then, suddenly, everything shifted.

A wave of dizziness overtook her, as though darkness had swallowed the white void. When her eyes fluttered open again, she found herself lying on a bed in an unfamiliar room. Nerin stared blankly at the ceiling—its surface pale but textured with faint patterns. She tried to sit up, but her body refused to obey. A throbbing headache pulsed behind her temples, and she collapsed weakly against the pillow.

"You're awake?" a voice asked.

Nerin turned toward the sound. Light spilled into the room as a figure stepped through the doorway—a girl in a sky-blue dress. Her expression hovered between quiet relief and a poorly concealed guilt, as though she’d been granted salvation but couldn’t yet forgive herself for needing it.

"Who… are you? Where… am I?" Nerin whispered, her voice frail as a moth’s wing.

The girl in blue approached slowly, her steps light. She sat in a chair beside the bed and smiled gently. "Don’t be afraid. No one here will harm you. I’m Ichinone Yuki. It’s nice to meet you."

Nerin glanced around the room. It was simple yet warm, with a clock ticking rhythmically in the corner. The scent of fresh flowers lingered in the air, soothing her frayed nerves.

"What should I call you?" Yuki asked.

"...Nerin," she murmured. Though confusion and fear still clawed at her chest, the room’s tranquility softened their edges. "Where is this place? How did I get here?"

Yuki took Nerin’s hand, her touch reassuring. "This is my home. I found you collapsed in the snow, barely breathing. That we can speak like this now… perhaps it’s a miracle fate allowed us."

Nerin tried to remember, but her mind was blank. Memories—if they’d ever existed—had been erased, leaving only shadows. "I… can’t recall anything. Not even who I am. When I try, all I feel is… terror—" Her voice broke. Tears pooled in her eyes as she shut them tightly.

Yuki brushed a strand of hair from Nerin’s face, her voice soft. "You don’t have to force yourself. Stay here as long as you need. When you’re ready to face the past, we’ll think about the future together."

"Thank you… Yuki-san." Nerin met her gaze, warmth blooming in her chest. She knew she was still too weak to move, too fragile to resist relying on this stranger’s kindness. Yet in this quiet room, with its ticking clock and floral serenity, she felt safe—an anchor in the storm.

Yuki stood, pausing at the door. "Rest now."

Alone again, Nerin stared at the ceiling. The clock’s ticking filled the silence, each second a reminder that time, unlike her memories, refused to freeze.
